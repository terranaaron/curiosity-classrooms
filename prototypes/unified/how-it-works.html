How it works

No mystery, no black box. Here's a typical engagement.

Every engagement is scoped to your division — but the shape is predictable, and you should know it before you ever pick up the phone.

Six steps, start to finish

01 · FREE

Intro call

Thirty minutes, no pitch — booked directly on the calendar. Bring the thing you're wrestling with. If I'm not the right help, I'll say so and point you somewhere better.

02 · $150, WAIVED ON SIGNING

Planning session

Sixty minutes of real work on your situation — your data, your calendar, your constraints. If an engagement is signed, the fee comes off the invoice.

03

Kickoff

One meeting with your sponsor and the people who'll live the work — coordinator, principal, teacher leads. We agree on scope, goals, who decides what, and how often you hear from me.

04

Listen first

Before advice: interviews, classroom time, and a look at the data and materials. Teachers get heard before anything gets prescribed.

05

The work, with checkpoints

Courses and coaching in roughly six-week cycles — every teacher on a coaching caseload meets with me one-on-one first. A standing check-in with your sponsor, monthly at minimum, with short written updates that land before your own leadership meetings.

06

Closeout

A final report with findings and recommendations, delivered in time to shape next year's plan and budget — plus every artifact from along the way. It's yours to keep.

A school-year engagement, month by month

August

Kickoff with your sponsor. The year gets mapped around your testing windows and professional-learning days; baseline data pull begins.

September

Fall professional-learning day. One-on-one meetings with every teacher on the coaching caseload — before any observation happens.

October – November

Coaching cycle one: observe, debrief, adjust. First checkpoint memo to your desk. Fall retake and EOC windows stay untouched.

December

Quiet by design — exams and holidays. A short winter progress note goes to leadership.

January

Coaching cycle two begins, scheduled around the winter testing window.

February – March

Cycle two wraps; the spring professional-learning day lands before testing prep takes over the building.

April – May protected

Spring SOL window. Your teachers' attention belongs to their students — light-touch support only, while the year's evidence gets gathered for the report.

June

Closeout: the final report and recommendations, timed to inform next year's plan — and next year's budget line.

Two promises about working together

THE CALENDAR IS SACRED

Your testing windows are protected

SOL windows, retakes, exam weeks — the engagement bends around your calendar, never the reverse. That gets mapped at kickoff, in writing.

NO SURPRISES

You'll never wonder where things stand

The communication cadence is agreed at kickoff — and every checkpoint produces something written you can forward to whoever asks.
